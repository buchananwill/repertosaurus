> Next we want to look at how we can lift it up from being purely a logger, to a nudger and a habit

> 1. Some kind of randomizer to suggest songs to practise. Stop the routine from getting stale.

>    uninspiring. We could do better.
> 5. As well as hottest/coldest, a facility to prioritize or prescribe how often to work on certain
>    repertoire. So the list doesn't just show most/least practised, it can show triage, which is a
>    combination of least practised and most needing practice. This could be fed via the "feel"
>    rating noted on a session. When I finish or start a practice session, I don't want to think in
>    cold, objective data about how soon I should practise that piece again. I want to record at
>    most: 1) Is the song a priority right now (e.g. an upcoming gig)? 2) How confident would I be
>    to gig it tomorrow? Those two metrics could be ranked 0-3: not at all, somewhat, certainly,
>    exceptionally. That gives enough data to relatively triage songs, and the triage view is one
>    ranking order amongst the others. Priority/Confidence also could be in either order, giving

## Decisions

The user ruled on 2026-09-23. These are **their decisions**, recorded here until the vision and
spec documents carry them:

- **D1. Confidence and priority both attach to a song–performer–role relationship**, the same
  `(song, performer, instrument)` triple as a `song_performer` row. The user's words: "I can be
  confident about the guitar part for a song, but not the vocal. I can be required to learn a
  song's guitar part, and ideally its vocal or backing vocal, but that be optional." (This
  supersedes my suggestion that priority attach to the song as a whole.)
- **D2. Both are sticky: they stay until manually changed.** Neither lapses.
- **D3. Both are edited on a view separate from logging**, "similar to the toggles for the
  performer roles". Neither goes on the tap path, because the one-tap loop is "critical". (This
  supersedes my suggestion that confidence replace feel on the long-press.)
- **D4. The control is a segmented `[0, 1, 2, 3]`, one tap per change. No dropdown**: "that makes
  two clicks what should be one."
- **D5. A pastel colour ramp, suggested as `[red, light red, light blue, blue]`, and the ramp is a
  user setting in the menu**: "some may want 'danger red to go green', others 'cold blue to red
  hot'."
- **D6. Commit and push the work so far.**
- **D7. Write these ideas up as a design vision first,** following the schema the user's other
  project uses (`D:\Coding\resort_game\PistePerfect_5_7\Notes\_schema.md`): "We capture the intent
  and aesthetics before we get lost in the details."

- **D8. Committed and pushed (per D6)** as `8dc64d5` on a new branch `feature/repertoire-editing`,
  tracking origin. **`main` was not moved** and is still at `86c70c2`. I branched rather than
  committing to the default branch; fast-forwarding `main` is the user's call.
- **D9. The vision is written:** [from-logger-to-nudger.md](../vision/from-logger-to-nudger.md),
  in a new `Notes/vision/` domain. It is `speculative` until the user moves it to `active`.
  `_schema.md` gains the `product` area and Piste Perfect's design-vision status set
  (`speculative | active | rejected | superseded`, moved only by the user). Only that part of the
  Piste Perfect schema was adopted. Its roadmaps, `part_of` / `depends_on` edges, spec status
  lifecycle and `check` tooling were **not** adopted; that is a larger migration of every existing
  note, and it is offered to the user rather than assumed.
- **D10. The vision states two of my proposals as direction, to be confirmed by the user when they
  read it:**
  - suggestions lean towards need, with pure shuffle kept as an option;
  - the timer is an alternative way to log, never a replacement for the tap.

- **D11. The vision's premise is corrected by the user: support, not direction.** I had written
  that musicians "want to be told what to play next". The user: "no, they want *support in
  sustaining a long term routine*. People come to creative hobbies for freedom from mandates. We're
  not trying to impose, we're trying to assist." Applied to the vision in four places:
  - the opening now says "supporting", not "shaping";
  - a new principle: **the app assists, never imposes**;
  - Suggest is reframed as an idea the musician asks for, that costs nothing to decline, with
    nothing recording that a suggestion was passed over;
  - "Not a mandate" leads "What this is not".

  **This is a standing test for every spec in this arc: would the musician experience this feature
  as an obligation?** If so, it changes shape. It is the counterpart to "the one tap is sacred".

- **D12. The suggester is tunable, and skips are a user setting (user, 2026-09-23).** This amends
  my D11 wording, "nothing records that a suggestion was passed over". The user: "I think that's
  missing an opportunity." Two settings:
  1. **How random:** a sliding scale from equal weighting of every song to weighting by triage,
     hotness, coldness and similar signals.
  2. **Skips counted or discarded.** When counted, repeated skips build weight, so the song is
     suggested gradually more often. When discarded, skipping leaves no record ("the liberation of
     just skipping it").

  The vision's Suggest section is rewritten accordingly. **Skips are recorded only when the user
  opted in.** This refines D11's test rather than contradicting it: a feature that is support for
  one musician and a burden for another becomes a setting.

- **D13. The randomness control is a radar chart (user, 2026-09-23)**, replacing D12's "sliding
  scale". The user's words: "zero weighting of the randomness inputs is pure random, increasing the
  radii less random in configurable proportions."
  - Each spoke is one weighting input (priority, confidence, coldness, hotness, counted skips).
  - Every radius at zero is pure shuffle.
  - The polygon's size says how random the suggester is; its shape says the proportions.

  Written into the vision's Suggest section. Open for the spec:
  - which spokes exist;
  - what coldness and hotness mean when both are non-zero;
  - whether the skips spoke shows only when skips are counted.

- **D14. Feel is kept and moves to the 0–3 scale (user, 2026-09-23):** "worth keeping, but
  change to [0,3] to match the triage ratings." It stays a per-session impression on long-press
  beside the sticky confidence rating, with the same four labels and the same colour ramp. It is
  written into the vision.

  Consequences for the spec, stated so they are not rediscovered:
  - **Stored feel values cannot be rewritten.** `practice_event` is immutable and append-only
    (data-model 7–8), and a union merge would never propagate an edit.
  - Today's values are **unlabelled numbers 1–3**: the FeelSheet shows bare chips, and the undo
    snackbar says "feel 2". So the natural reading is to **keep every stored number and read it
    on the new scale** (1 → somewhat, 2 → certainly, 3 → exceptionally), with 0 newly available
    for "not at all". That is my proposal; the spec confirms it with the user. The alternative, a
    read-time remap, would disagree with the stored number forever.
  - **The `CHECK (feel BETWEEN 1 AND 3)` on `practice_event` must widen to 0–3.** SQLite cannot
    alter a CHECK, so this is a table rebuild in the schema-3 migration, which also has to keep
    the table append-only in effect. Validation in `RepertosaurusRepository.log` (`feel in 1..3`)
    widens with it.
  - Check whether `tools/import/` emits `feel` from the workbook. If it does, the Python side's
    range must agree (CLAUDE.md's two-implementations rule).

- **D15. Counted skips attach to the song–performer–role, in a separate table (user, 2026-09-23).**
  The user named it `suggestion_skip_count`.

  **Conflict raised with the user, not yet resolved.** Stored as a running count, the table would
  break two recorded data-model rules:
  - decision 11: only two merge rules, append-only by union on id, everything else last-write-wins
    per row;
  - decision 14: "No table may depend on insertion order or a monotonic counter."

  Under last-write-wins, two devices that each record a skip lose one increment.

  **My proposal:** an append-only table with one row per skip (`suggestion_skip`, with a random id,
  the triple, `created_at` and `device_id`), counted at read time. It merges by union like
  `practice_event`. That joins the *existing* append-only rule, so it does not add a third rule, and
  decision 14 holds. It also answers "does it fade" cheaply: the weight can be "skips since this
  part was last practised", which resets when the part is played, with no write needed.

  **Also: the vision cited "D2"**, a journal decision number, which a reader of the vision cannot
  resolve. The user asked what it was. **A vision must never cite journal numbering**; it says the
  rule in words. This is the vision-side twin of F31. Fixed.

- **D16. Resolves D15: skips are stored append-only, one row per skip, counted at read time
  (user, 2026-09-23: "Yes that's fine").** The table is `suggestion_skip`: a random id, the
  `(song, performer, instrument)` triple, `created_at`, `device_id`. It merges by union on id,
  joining data-model decision 11's existing append-only rule, and decision 14 holds.

  **Volume is explicitly not a concern.** The user: "We don't have a realistic risk of any kind of
  data explosion, because this is counting human behaviour, not HF trading." **No compaction or
  pruning mechanism is to be designed.**

  **Still open:** whether the weight fades. The user's "yes" answered the storage question, not
  the fading one. My proposed default for the spec: "skips since this part was last practised",
  which resets on play with no write. It is stated as a proposal, to confirm when the suggester spec
  is read.

- **D17. When skips are counted: an optional skip-count display and an undo window (user,
  2026-09-23).**
  - **(a) Optionally show on the suggestion how many times this part has been skipped since it
    was last practised.** The user wants this so they "change [their] mind conscientiously" on
    seeing, say, 20 skips.
  - **(b) A debounce window in which a skip can be undone.**
  - This also **settles D16's open fading question: the count is "since the part was last
    practised"**, which confirms my proposed default.
  - Written into the vision's Suggest section.

  Spec note, so it is not rediscovered: **the undo should be implemented as "write the skip only
  once the window closes"**, not as write-then-void. `suggestion_skip` is append-only and has no
  void table, and a skip taken back "leaves no trace" per the vision. The cost: a skip is lost if
  the app is killed inside the window. That is the right way round for a feature whose failure mode
  must be "less nagging", never "more".
